Personal Project

A Personal Gin Course, Built for an Audience of One

A personal gin course for my partner — and a solo experiment in building learning experiences that don't exist in any standard authoring tool.

Role
Sole Designer, Developer & Subject Matter Researcher
Timeline
10 weeks (March – May 2026)
Impact
Self-taught vibe coding, transferred to colleague

My partner enjoys gin. Really enjoys it — the history, the botanicals, the way a good G&T comes together. I wanted to build them something that went deeper than the label on the bottle: a proper walk through the category, the production, the science, and the culture behind what they were drinking.

The second reason was more selfish. A colleague had introduced me to vibe coding — building custom interactive elements through plain-language conversation with Claude — and I wanted to prove it out on a real project. Something with actual stakes: a real learner, real subject matter, and a design that wouldn't compromise on what the learning called for.

The audience: an audience of one. Someone who loves the category and deserves a course built at the depth they'd actually enjoy.

A typical authoring tool like Articulate Rise gives you a fixed interaction library — accordions, flashcards, labeled graphics, standard knowledge checks. Useful, but none of them could carry the moments this course needed: a pH-responsive colour shift demonstrating butterfly pea flower chemistry, a botanical specimen journal styled as a field naturalist's notebook, a client-conversation stepper with revealed coaching moves, a history timeline with a conditional callout debunking a 350-year-old myth.

The instructional design called for interactions that didn't exist in any standard library. The challenge wasn't whether to build custom — it was whether a solo designer with zero prior coding experience could pull it off.

Six chapters, ~15 minutes, structured around a naturalist's field-journal metaphor — Latin chapter subtitles, botanical illustrations, specimen cards, archive-gold typography on deep green. Backward design drove the structure (learning objectives before content), Bloom's leveled the knowledge checks, action mapping shaped the final two chapters into real scenarios, and the Made to Stick SUCCESs framework structured the history chapter around surprise and concrete story.

Twelve custom widgets were vibe-coded in plain-language conversation with Claude, refined visually, deployed to GitHub Pages, and embedded via iframe — letting the design lead the technology rather than the reverse. The full design system was documented in a reference sheet that doubles as a job aid, with a WCAG AA-compliant palette and intentional typography pairing (IM Fell English for headings, Inter for body). The vibe coding approach proved transferable: I taught it to a colleague before the course was finished.

Building this confirmed that vibe coding rewards patience and attention to detail — the widgets that worked best were the ones I refused to ship until every interaction flowed cleanly. I'd still build content and widgets in parallel; seeing what a learner will actually experience is how I know if the design is working. What I'm testing next is whether the same approach holds up in other tools beyond Rise — Claude Design, Mindsmith, and Chameleon Creator.

The course was piloted by my partner and friends.
