1What you'll get

Job searching takes a lot of energy. This setup handles the repetitive parts so you can put that energy into applying and preparing.

When you're done, you'll have:

  • Your own role tracker. A page that saves every role you're watching and where it stands, from "New" to "Offer." Each role keeps its full description, how you applied and your notes from each interview.
  • A fit check for any posting. Paste a job description, and Claude compares it with the kind of role you want: pay, location, role type and deal-breakers.
  • An automatic job search. Claude checks for new roles on a schedule you choose, adds good matches to your tracker, and sends you a short summary.

A note on what to expect: this saves you time, but it can't promise interviews or offers, and it will sometimes miss a role or flag one that isn't a fit. You make every decision, and Claude never applies or sends messages for you.

2Before you start

A few checks to make before you start, so setup goes smoothly:

  • A paid Claude plan (Pro or higher). Saving your tracker and running the automatic search both need a paid plan, and plans and prices vary by country. Plan details last checked October 31, 2026.
  • A regular chat, not incognito. Incognito chats can't build your tracker because they can't create files or run code, and they can't be reopened once closed.
  • "Artifacts" and "Code execution and file creation" turned on. Claude needs both to build your tracker, and they're usually on already. Some versions of Claude call the second one “Create and edit files.” To check, go to Settings → Capabilities.
  • About 15 minutes. You'll answer about 10 short questions.

On the free plan? You can still use a lighter version by running a search prompt yourself and keeping track of roles in a Google Sheet or a simple list, though you won't get the saved tracker or the automatic daily search. The free-plan prompt is coming soon.

3Your privacy choices

Make these choices before you paste the prompt, since some only apply from the start of a chat.

Before you start

  1. How your chats may be used. You choose whether your chats help improve Claude. If you'd rather they don't, turn off the Model Improvement setting in your Privacy Settings (claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls) before you paste the prompt.
  2. Memory. Memory is on by default. To keep this setup out of memory, turn it off for this chat before you send your first message, or turn off "Generate memory from chats" in the Memory section of your settings (on the mobile app, go to Settings → Capabilities).

After setup

  • Your tracker starts private. "Artifacts start private to you. Nothing is shared until you share it."
  • Shared links show what's saved. Your tracker saves your roles, notes and Settings, so think before sharing its link.
  • Your data is yours. Select Back up all anytime to download every role and its details as a spreadsheet, and review or delete memories later in the Memory section of your Claude settings.

From Claude's Help Center and Privacy Center. Links are in the Help tab.

5Get your answers ready

Claude will ask about these during setup, so thinking them over first makes it faster. Nothing has to be perfect, and you can change everything later in Settings.

Needed to start

  • Your field and job titles. Even one title works. A few similar titles (like "Project Coordinator" and "Program Coordinator") find more roles.
  • Where you'd work: remote, hybrid or on-site; your home city; and any places you'd rule out.

Helpful, but you can skip

  • Level: entry, mid, senior or lead, or director and up.
  • Managing people: whether you're open to it, and how many.
  • Travel: how much is okay.
  • Pay floor: your lowest acceptable base pay. You can set different amounts for remote and in-office roles.
  • Dream companies: up to 10 to check every time.
  • Deal-breakers: role types, requirements or industries to skip.
  • Time zone, and what time you'd like results.
  • Network tab (optional): a place to track people who could help, like referrals, references and job leads. Say no if you'd rather keep it simple.

If you skip any of these, Claude won't filter on it, and it flags anything you might want to know.

6Build your tracker
  1. Copy the starter prompt below.
  2. Open a new regular chat in Claude (the "Open Claude" button does this) and paste it in.
  3. Answer Claude's questions. It asks one or two at a time, about 10 in all.
  4. Check the summary. Claude lists your answers and anything you skipped. Change anything that's off.
  5. Open your new tracker. Claude builds it and sends you a link. Open the Stats, Roles, Badges, Network and Settings tabs and the Add a role button and check that each one matches what you asked for, then bookmark it.
  6. Approve your search schedule. Claude tells you exactly what it plans to schedule and waits for your OK before anything runs.

<skip_rules>
If I skip a question, use these rules so my search widens rather than narrows:

- Level: no filter.
- Managing people: no filter; flag roles with direct reports.
- Travel: no limit; flag any travel requirement.

Tips

  • Not sure about a question? Type "skip" and set it later in Settings. Claude won't filter on it in the meantime.
  • "Not confirmed:" means it's a guess. Claude starts a sentence with "Not confirmed:" when it isn't sure, so check those details yourself before you rely on them.
  • Try the Demo tab first if you want to know what you're building.
  • Want a timeline strip for the dates that matter to your search? It's optional, and you can add one later in step 8.
7Your daily routine

About 10–15 minutes a day keeps things moving:

  1. Read your search summary. It arrives at the time you chose. Start with the "Apply today" line, which lists the newest strong matches.
  2. Open your tracker. It opens on Stats, which shows how many roles are new to review and how many follow-ups are due. Select new to review, then move each match to Interested or Passed.
  3. Apply to your top picks. Roles posted in the last day or two are worth applying to first. Then move them to Applied, where an optional short box asks how you applied, which resume you used and any answers you submitted.
  4. Update anything that changed. Got a screen or interview? Move the role forward, then select Add interview to note who you met, what they asked and the next step. Got a rejection? Mark it Rejected and note how you heard.
  5. Reach out to one person (if you set up Network). Check who's due for a follow-up, and send one short note. A referral often gets more attention than an application on its own.
  6. Check fit on roles you find yourself. Found something on LinkedIn or through a friend? Paste the description into Add role.

Tip: your notes are your memory. A short line like "spoke with recruiter, next step panel" saves a lot of digging later.

Once a week: select Back up all in your tracker. It saves a spreadsheet copy of every role, so your history is safe even if something happens to the page.

8Optional: add a timeline strip

If it would help to know how much time you have, you can add a thin strip to the bottom of your tracker's Stats tab that shows a few dates you choose, such as your last day of work, the day your paid time off runs out, or when you expect severance or another income to end. It stays hidden until you add your first date, and you can remove it whenever you like.

What it saves: only dates, short labels and an optional note for each one. It never asks for dollar amounts, so the strip shows nothing about your finances if you share your tracker's link. You choose every date yourself, and the strip never tells you when to change your search.

9Changing things later

You can change your search whenever your situation changes.

To change what your search is based on, open Settings in your tracker: job titles, level, work style, locations, travel, pay floor, dream companies and deal-breakers. Changes take effect on your next search, and the fit check uses them right away.

To change when or how often it runs, ask Claude in a chat. For example:

  • "Change my job search to weekday mornings at 7 AM."
  • "Switch my search pace to Light."
  • "Pause my job search."

To stop entirely, ask Claude to delete your scheduled search. Your tracker and its history stay, so you can come back to them anytime.

If your version of Claude shows a Scheduled item in the left sidebar, you can also select it to see your searches, pause one, edit its instructions or timing, or delete it.

Tip: if your results include too many poor fits or too few roles overall, check Settings first, since a missing title or a slightly high pay floor is usually the cause.

10How I set mine up

Here's what works for me. Use it as a starting point, not a rulebook.

  • Pace: Full-time search. Two searches every morning, weekends included, about 25 minutes apart.
  • First search: checks my short list of dream companies only, so I don't miss a role at a place I really want to work.
  • Second search: checks the wider set of job sites and opens with an "Apply today" line for the freshest roles. My goal is to apply within a day of a posting going live.
  • Cleanup: twice a week, closed postings get marked Closed, so my active list only shows real openings.
  • Fit check: before I apply to anything I found myself, I paste it in and check the 4 basics: pay, location, role type and deal-breakers.
  • When I apply: I move the role to Applied and fill in the short application box: how I applied, which resume, and my answers. The full job description is already saved on its card, which helps because postings often disappear right when you need them for interview prep.
  • After each interview: I add it to the role's Interviews section right away, while it's fresh: who I met, what they asked, what went well and the next step.
  • When things change: I update the stage to whatever happened next, like Screen, Interview, Offer or Rejected, and add a short note. Over time, that shows me where things move and where they stall.
  • Backups: once a week I select Back up all, so I always have my own copy.

Job searching is a common target for scammers, including on real job sites like LinkedIn.

Warning signs

  • A recruiter reaches out unexpectedly, especially by text, WhatsApp or Telegram. Real employers don't recruit that way.
  • They email from a personal address (like @gmail.com) instead of a company account.
  • You get an offer or paperwork before a real interview.
  • They ask you to pay for equipment or training, or to deposit a check and send money back.
  • They ask for your ID, a government ID number (like a US Social Security number) or bank details before you're hired.
  • Pay is mentioned, but the actual work is vague.

Before you share anything

  • Ask about the job duties and pay first.
  • Hold off on identity and banking details until you've interviewed and been hired.
  • Contact the company using a website or phone number you know is real, not one the recruiter gave you.
  • Search the recruiter's or company's name with words like "scam" or "complaint."
  • Never pay to get a role.

If you think it's a scam

  • Stop replying, and report it to your country's fraud or consumer protection agency. In the US, that's ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and if the site is not taking reports when you try it, check back later and report the scam on the platform where it happened in the meantime.
  • Report the fake profile or message on the platform where it happened. LinkedIn, for example, has a built-in "Report" option.

How this setup helps: the daily search is set up to only add roles it found on employer pages and confirmed are open, and Claude never sends messages or applies for you. It's still worth checking anything that seems off.
