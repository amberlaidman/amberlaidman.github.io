Riding With No Driver — Module 5 of 6

What to Expect on a Ride

The part you actually came for.

{{ callbackLine }}

Quick context before we get in. “Self-driving” covers a lot — from cars with fancy cruise control that still need you watching the road, all the way to a car with genuinely no one driving. We mean that last one: the robotaxi. You don’t own it and you don’t drive it — you hail it like a rideshare, and it shows up empty.

Waymo is the one most people meet first, so that’s what we’ll ride through here. It isn’t the only one — Zoox is running in Las Vegas, and more are arriving in 2026 — but they all work much the same way.

01 / 06

Request it.

Open the app and request a ride, same as any rideshare. The app tells you when it’s about a minute out.

02 / 06

Spot it.

It pulls up on its own. Your initials appear on the dome on the car’s roof, so you know it’s yours.

03 / 06

Get in.

The car arrives locked — unlock it from the app, then open the door and climb in the back. The front seat’s empty — that’s normal now.

04 / 06

Start.

Buckle up and tap “Start ride” on the in-car screen. The car won’t move until you’re set.

05 / 06

During.

Sit back. A screen shows you what the car sees in real time. Music, temperature, and windows are yours. Need to stop early? Tap “Pull over” anytime.

06 / 06

Arrive.

It pulls over and parks. Open the doors from the screen, step out — and close the door behind you.

This part lets you look around the cabin in 360°. If motion makes you dizzy, you can skip straight to the written version below.

Prefer to read it? Here’s everything inside, point by point:

  • The screen.The in-car touchscreen shows your route and runs your trip — start the ride, pull over, play music, adjust the temperature, and reach Support.
  • Support.Tap Support on the screen or in the app any time, day or night, to reach a real person.
  • The empty driver’s seat.No one sits up front to drive. The driver’s seat stays empty the whole trip — and after a few minutes, you stop noticing.
  • Seatbelt.Buckle up before you start. Seatbelts are required for everyone, the whole ride.
  • Doors.The car arrives locked. Unlock from the app or the screen, then open the door. At your stop, open the doors from the screen — and remember to close them behind you.

Your call

You’re six minutes into the ride. The car slows down, signals, and pulls over to the curb — nowhere near your stop. The screen says it’s working on it.

What do you do?

{{ decFeedback }}

It’s built for exactly that. If the car hits a situation it’s unsure about, it does the safest thing — it slows or pulls over, instead of guessing. It can reach a remote support team who see what the car sees and help it read a tricky scene. They don’t drive it by remote control — the car stays in charge; they just help it understand. And you’re never stuck: one tap reaches a real person, any time, for any reason.

Empty seat, sure. But you’re not alone — and the car’s first instinct when in doubt is to stop, not to gamble.
